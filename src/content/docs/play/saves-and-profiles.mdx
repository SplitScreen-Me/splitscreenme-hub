## Where the saves go

It depends on the handler, because every game saves differently. There are three common cases:

| Case | Where | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Per player, in the Nucleus environment** | `C:\Users\<you>\NucleusCoop\...` | Each player has their own folder, separate from your normal saves |
| **Per player, in their own Documents** | A Documents folder per player, under the Nucleus environment | The same, for games that save in Documents |
| **The game's usual place** | Wherever the game normally saves | All copies share it: progress mixes with your solo game, so back it up first |

Games that use a Steam or Epic emulator (most handlers) also keep emulator saves per player, tied to that player's
Steam ID or nickname.

### Open or back up a player's save

In the library, open **Game options** on the game. When the handler declares its save and config folders, the
menu has entries such as **Open UserProfile Save Path**, **Open Document Save Path** or **Open Backup Folder**,
with one entry per player. Copy those folders somewhere safe before deleting anything.

**Delete UserProfile Config Path** (and its siblings) resets a player's settings for that game, which fixes many
"file not found" and wrong-resolution problems. It doesn't touch the other players.

<Callout type="warning" title="Before you clean up">

**Delete Game Content Folder** removes the prepared copies of the game, not the saves in your user folder. But
deleting `C:\Users\<you>\NucleusCoop` does remove the players' saves for the games that keep them there. Back it
up first.

</Callout>

[PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/) lists where most games keep their saves, which helps when a handler
uses the game's usual place.

### Play with your own save

Some games tie a save to the Steam ID of the player. Nucleus gives each player slot a default Steam ID, so player 1
doesn't load your own progress. To use it, set player 1's Steam ID to yours in **Settings › Players** (nicknames
and Steam IDs are edited per slot: player 1 always gets the first slot). Your Steam ID is the 17-digit number
in your Steam profile URL, or in **Account details** in Steam.

The same setting matters when you [connect two PCs](/doc/faq#29--can-you-play-splitscreenlan-in-different-pcs):
copies on different PCs need different Steam IDs to see each other.

### Documents in OneDrive

For games that save in Documents, Nucleus points the Documents folder of each player elsewhere during the session,
and puts it back when you close Nucleus Co-op (or the next time you start it, after a crash). If your Documents
folder is synced by OneDrive, this can fail. See
[A handler does not work](/doc/troubleshooting/handler-does-not-work#documents-in-onedrive).

## Profiles

A profile keeps a whole setup for one game: the layouts, the handler options, who plays where and with which
controller, and per-player settings such as nicknames, Steam IDs and audio outputs.

- **Save one.** In the lobby, turn on **Save a profile when the session starts**, then launch.
- **Use one.** Pick it from the profiles menu in the lobby header. Players drop into their places as they press a
  button. With **Start by itself** on, the session launches as soon as everyone has joined.
- **Change one.** **Manage profiles** shows the saved profiles of a game, with their players and settings, and lets
  you delete them. Profile settings also cover processor priority and affinity per player, and the delays between
  copies, for the few games that need them.

Profiles are saved in the `game profiles` folder of Nucleus Co-op. To turn them off and always use the global
settings, use **Settings › General › Disable game profiles**.
