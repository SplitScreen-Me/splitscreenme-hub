Nucleus Co-op turns one PC and one copy of a game into a split-screen session. It starts one copy of the game per
player, gives each copy its own controller, keyboard or mouse, connects the copies through the game's own LAN or
online mode, and lays the windows out on your screen.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check your PC">
    A 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 PC that runs the game comfortably, with enough memory for one copy per player. The
    details are in [System requirements](/doc/getting-started/system-requirements).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Download and unzip Nucleus Co-op">
    Get the [latest release](/download) and extract it into its own folder, for example `C:\NucleusCo-op`. No
    installer, no account. [Install Nucleus Co-op](/doc/getting-started/install) explains where to put it and what
    to do when Windows or your antivirus complains.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a game">
    Open `NucleusCoop.exe`. **Found on this PC** lists the Steam and Epic games you already have that have a handler
    on the hub: tick them and add them in one go. For anything else, open **Get games** and install the handler from
    the catalogue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Everyone joins">
    Pick the game, choose **Set up session**, and hand out the controllers. Each player presses a button to join and
    takes the next free place on the screen. Keyboards and mice join with a key press.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Launch">
    Press **Launch**. Nucleus opens one copy per player and places the windows. Then follow the handler notes to
    connect the copies, usually by hosting a game in one window and joining it from the others.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The full walkthrough, with what each screen does, is in [Your first session](/doc/getting-started/first-session).

<Callout type="warning" title="One thing Nucleus can't do">

Nucleus Co-op needs a game that already has some form of LAN or online multiplayer, or a mod that adds one. It
can't turn a single-player game into a co-op game. Check the game on the [hub](/hub) first: if it's there, someone
has made it work.

</Callout>
